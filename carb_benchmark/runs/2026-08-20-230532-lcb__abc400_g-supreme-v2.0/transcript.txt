### Summary of Work

1. **Problem Analysis & Reduction**:
   - For any pairing of $K$ disjoint pairs $(a_m, b_m)$, the price is $\sum_{m=1}^K \max(X_{a_m}+X_{b_m}, Y_{a_m}+Y_{b_m}, Z_{a_m}+Z_{b_m})$.
   - By LP duality and structural properties of disjoint pairing, evaluating a pair $(a_m, b_m)$ at dimension $c \in \{X, Y, Z\}$ means assigning both $a_m$ and $b_m$ to dimension $c$.
   - Thus, any selection of $K$ pairs corresponds to choosing disjoint sets of cakes $U_X, U_Y, U_Z$ of sizes $2k_X, 2k_Y, 2k_Z$ respectively (where $k_X + k_Y + k_Z = K$), maximizing $\sum_{i \in U_X} X_i + \sum_{j \in U_Y} Y_j + \sum_{l \in U_Z} Z_l$.

2. **Algorithm Design & Optimization**:
   - The unconstrained optimal allocation of $2K$ items is found by selecting the top $2K$ cakes maximizing $\max(X_i, Y_i, Z_i)$.
   - Due to the M-concavity of the value function on the allocation simplex, the optimal parity-constrained (even) bucket counts $(2k_X, 2k_Y, 2k_Z)$ lie within a small distance $d \le 3$ of the unconstrained optimum.
   - For candidate even triples near the unconstrained optimum, we compute the maximum value using augmenting paths in the 4-vertex residual graph ($\{0, X, Y, Z\}$).

3. **Verification & Performance**:

   - Verified against all sample test cases.
