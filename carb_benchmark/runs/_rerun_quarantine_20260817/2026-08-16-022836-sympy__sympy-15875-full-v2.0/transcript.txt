### Summary of Changes

#### Root Cause
In `sympy/core/add.py`, `Add._eval_is_zero` previously used boolean flags `im` and `im_or_z` to track imaginary terms, returning `False` whenever `im` was `True` and `im_or_z` was `False`. This logic assumed that any expression with imaginary terms had a non-zero imaginary part, incorrectly returning `False` for expressions with multiple imaginary terms that cancel out (such as `-2*I + (1 + I)**2` where `(1 + I)**2` simplifies to `2*I`).

#### Solution
- Updated [add.py](file:///E:/RofU/Supreme/carb_workspaces/sympy__sympy-15875/sympy/core/add.py#L532-L565) to collect imaginary terms into `im_I` (scaling by `I` to get real coefficients), matching the pattern in `_eval_is_imaginary`.
- When the real part `b.is_zero` is `True`, `Add._eval_is_zero` now evaluates `self.func(*im_I).is_zero` to determine whether the imaginary part sums to zero.
- Added regression tests in [test_complex.py](file:///E:/RofU/Supreme/carb_workspaces/sympy__sympy-15875/sympy/core/tests/test_complex.py#L222-L228) verifying that `(-2*I + (1 + I)**2).is_zero` evaluates to `True`.
