### Method Explanation
1. **Mathematical Reduction**:
   Any positive integer $b$ can be factored into $b = 2^k \cdot c$, where $c \ge 1$ is odd and $k \ge 0$.
   Thus, $X = 2^a \cdot b^2 = 2^a \cdot (2^k \cdot c)^2 = 2^{a+2k} \cdot c^2$.
   Setting $E = a + 2k$, since $a \ge 1$ and $k \ge 0$, $E$ can be any integer $E \ge 1$.
   Hence, $X$ is a good integer if and only if $X = 2^E \cdot c^2$ for some $E \ge 1$ and odd $c \ge 1$.

2. **Counting**:
   For a fixed $E \ge 1$, we need $2^E \cdot c^2 \le N \implies c^2 \le \lfloor N / 2^E \rfloor$.
   Let $M = \lfloor N / 2^E \rfloor$. The maximum odd integer $c$ is $c_{\text{max}} = \lfloor \sqrt{M} \rfloor$.
   The number of positive odd integers in $[1, c_{\text{max}}]$ is $\lfloor (c_{\text{max}} + 1) / 2 \rfloor$.
   Summing this over all $1 \le E \le \log_2(N)$ gives the total count of good integers in $O(\log N)$ time.
