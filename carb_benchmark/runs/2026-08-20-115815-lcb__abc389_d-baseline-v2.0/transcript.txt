### Explanation
1. **Condition for Containment**:
   A $1 \times 1$ square centered at $(i, j)$ has vertices at $(i \pm 0.5, j \pm 0.5)$. The maximum distance from the origin to any of its four vertices occurs at $(|i| + 0.5, |j| + 0.5)$.
   The square is completely contained within a circle of radius $R$ centered at $(0, 0)$ if and only if:
   $$(|i| + 0.5)^2 + (|j| + 0.5)^2 \le R^2 \iff (2|i| + 1)^2 + (2|j| + 1)^2 \le 4R^2$$

2. **Algorithm**:
   - We iterate $i \ge 0$ while maintaining $j \ge 0$ using a two-pointer approach such that $(2i + 1)^2 + (2j + 1)^2 \le 4R^2$.
   - For a given $i$, the number of valid values for $j \in \mathbb{Z}$ is $(2j_{max} + 1)$.
   - Due to symmetry:
     - For $i = 0$, $i$ can only be 0 (1 choice), contributing $(2j_{max} + 1)$ to the answer.
     - For $i > 0$, $i$ can be positive or negative (2 choices), contributing $2 \times (2j_{max} + 1)$ to the answer.
   - The total number of iterations for the outer loop and inner decrements of $j$ is at most $2R \le 2 \times 10^6$, running in under 0.3 seconds.
