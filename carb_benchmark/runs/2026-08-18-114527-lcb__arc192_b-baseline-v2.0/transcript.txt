### Game Analysis & Solution Strategy

1. **Winning Condition**:
   - The game ends as soon as $|S| = N$. The player who performs the move that adds the $N$-th unique index to $S$ wins.
   - If $|S| = N - 1$, the player to move can immediately choose the sole remaining unvisited index (which always has capacity $A_i \ge 1$) to reach $|S| = N$ and win.
   - Hence, leaving $|S| = N - 1$ to your opponent is a losing move. Both players will avoid increasing $|S|$ to $N - 1$ until forced to do so.

2. **Case Analysis**:
   - **$N = 1$**: Fennec picks index $1$ on turn 1, $|S| = 1 = N$, and **Fennec wins**.
   - **$N = 2$**: Fennec is forced on turn 1 to pick one element, making $|S| = 1 = N - 1$. Snuke picks the second element on turn 2 and **Snuke wins**.
   - **$N = 3$**: The players stall on the first element added to $S$ until all its extra operations are depleted. If Fennec picks an index $i$ with $A_i$ odd on turn 1, the stalling phase lasts an even number of moves, forcing Snuke to break the barrier to $|S| = 2$, after which Fennec picks the 3rd element and wins. Thus, **Fennec wins if at least one $A_i$ is odd**, otherwise **Snuke wins**.
   - **$N \ge 4$**: The game reduces to the parity of $\sum_{i=1}^N A_i$. **Fennec wins if $\sum A_i$ is odd**, and **Snuke wins if $\sum A_i$ is even**.
