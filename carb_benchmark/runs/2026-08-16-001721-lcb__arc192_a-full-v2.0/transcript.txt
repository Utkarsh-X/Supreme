An analysis of the problem leads to the following key structural insights:

### Structural Analysis
1. **Operation Mechanics**:
   - Choosing `S[i..i+2] == "ARC"` sets $A_i = 1$ and $A_{i+1} = 1$. The character at $i+1$ is `'R'`.
   - Choosing `S[i..i+2] == "CRA"` sets $A_i = 1$ and $A_{i+1} = 1$. The character at $i+1$ is `'R'`.
   - In both operations, the middle character must be `'R'`, and the operation covers positions $i$ and $i+1$ (the left character and middle character `'R'`).

2. **Feasible Operation Patterns**:
   - Let $M \subseteq \{1, \dots, N\}$ be the set of indices chosen to be the middle `'R'` of an operation.
   - For an operation centered at $m \in M$, positions $m-1$ and $m$ are set to $1$. Thus, position $x$ is covered if and only if $x \in M$ or $x+1 \in M$.
   - Two operations cannot be at adjacent positions, so $M$ must be cyclically non-adjacent ($\forall m \in M, m+1 \notin M$).
   - A position $S_m = \text{'R'}$ requires its neighbors $\{S_{m-1}, S_{m+1}\} = \{\text{'A'}, \text{'C'}\}$. This places edge inequalities on non-'R' positions of $S$.
   - Analyzing the graph of non-'R' positions shows that any non-adjacent set $M$ is realizable as a valid string $S$ **except** when $N \equiv 2 \pmod 4$ and $M$ consists of all odd positions $f_1 = (1, 0, 1, 0, \dots)$ or all even positions $f_2 = (0, 1, 0, 1, \dots)$, which would force an odd cycle in the neighborhood graph.

3. **Dynamic Programming**:
   - We construct a sequence $m \in \{0, 1\}^N$ representing which operations are active:
     - No two adjacent $1$s: $(m_i, m_{i+1}) \neq (1, 1)$.
     - For $A_i = 0$, at least one of $m_i, m_{i+1}$ must be $1$: $(m_i, m_{i+1}) \neq (0, 0)$.
     - For $A_i = 1$, $(0, 0)$ is allowed.
   - For $N \equiv 2 \pmod 4$, $m$ must not equal $f_1$ or $f_2$, which is equivalent to requiring at least one $(0, 0)$ transition.
   - A simple DP of state $O(N)$ tracks reachability and whether a $(0, 0)$ transition has been used.
