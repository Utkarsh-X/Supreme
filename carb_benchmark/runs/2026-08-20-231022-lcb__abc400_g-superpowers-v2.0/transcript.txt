### Solution Explanation

1. **Mathematical Equivalence**:
   Given $K$ pairs of cakes $(a_i, b_i)$, each pair contributes $\max(X_{a_i} + X_{b_i}, Y_{a_i} + Y_{b_i}, Z_{a_i} + Z_{b_i})$. If a pair achieves its maximum at coordinate $C \in \{X, Y, Z\}$, both cakes $a_i$ and $b_i$ contribute their $C$-attribute. 
   Thus, choosing $K$ pairs is equivalent to choosing $2K$ distinct cakes and assigning each to category $X$, $Y$, or $Z$ such that the counts of cakes assigned to $X$, $Y$, and $Z$ (denoted $S_X, S_Y, S_Z$) are all **even** numbers summing to $2K$.

2. **Algorithm**:
   - For each cake $i$, calculate $V_i = \max(X_i, Y_i, Z_i)$ and mark its preferred category $cat_i = \arg\max(X_i, Y_i, Z_i)$.
   - Sort the cakes in descending order of $V_i$ and select the top $2K$ cakes (`chosen`).
   - If the category counts in `chosen` are all even, this unconstrained assignment is already valid and optimal.
   - If two category counts are odd, we adjust at most 2 item assignments (via candidate swaps/reassignments between `chosen` and `unchosen` cakes) to restore parity while maximizing the total value.

3. **Complexity**:
   - **Time Complexity**: $O(N \log N)$ per test case for sorting, and $O(1)$ candidate moves evaluation. Total time $\sum O(N \log N) \approx 0.05$ seconds.
   - **Space Complexity**: $O(N)$ for storing cakes.
