I have implemented the solution for finding the number of $1 \times 1$ squares completely contained inside a circle of radius $R$ in [`main.py`](file:///E:/RofU/Supreme/carb_workspaces/lcb__abc389_d/main.py).

### Method Explanation
1. A square centered at $(i, j)$ has 4 corners at coordinates $(\pm (|i| + 0.5), \pm (|j| + 0.5))$. The square is completely inside the circle of radius $R$ if and only if its farthest corner is at a distance of at most $R$ from the origin:
   $$(|i| + 0.5)^2 + (|j| + 0.5)^2 \le R^2$$
2. Multiplying by 4 to avoid floating-point operations:
   $$(2|i| + 1)^2 + (2|j| + 1)^2 \le 4R^2$$
3. Let $u = |i|$ and $v = |j|$. For each non-negative integer $u \ge 0$, we find the maximum non-negative integer $v \ge 0$ satisfying the inequality using an $O(R)$ two-pointer approach with $O(1)$ incremental term updates.
4. Each valid pair $(u, v)$ contributes to the overall symmetry count:
   - $(u=0, v=0)$ contributes 1 square.
   - $(u=0, v>0)$ contributes 2 squares: $(0, \pm v)$.
   - $(u>0, v=0)$ contributes 2 squares: $(\pm u, 0)$.
   - $(u>0, v>0)$ contributes 4 squares: $(\pm u, \pm v)$.
