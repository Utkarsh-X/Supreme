### Method Explanation

1. **Alkane Subgraph Structure**:
   An alkane subgraph $T[S]$ consists of a set $K$ of internal vertices (each having degree 4 in $T[S]$) and a set of leaves attached to vertices in $K$ (each having degree 1 in $T[S]$).
   - Because $T[S]$ is a tree, $K$ must be a connected subtree of $T$.
   - Any vertex $u \in K$ must have degree $d_T(u) \ge 4$ in the original tree $T$, and degree $d_K(u) \le 4$ in the subtree $K$.
   - Each vertex $u \in K$ requires $4 - d_K(u)$ leaves from $T \setminus K$. Since $d_T(u) \ge 4$, there are always at least $4 - d_K(u)$ disjoint leaves available in $T \setminus K$.
   - For a set of $m = |K|$ internal vertices, the total number of vertices in the alkane subgraph $T[S]$ is $3m + 2$.

2. **Algorithm**:
   - Filter all candidate internal vertices $V_{\ge 4} = \{v \in V(T) : d_T(v) \ge 4\}$. If $V_{\ge 4} = \emptyset$, output `-1`.
   - Consider the forest induced by $V_{\ge 4}$.
   - For each connected component, root it arbitrarily and use bottom-up dynamic programming:
     - `dp[u]` computes the maximum size of a valid subtree of internal vertices rooted at $u$ when $u$'s parent in $T[V_{\ge 4}]$ is also included (so $u$ can pick at most 3 children).
     - `ans[u]` computes the maximum size of a valid subtree of internal vertices rooted at $u$ when $u$ is the root of $K$ (so $u$ can pick at most 4 children).
   - Find the maximum number of internal vertices $m = \max_u ans[u]$.
   - Output $3m + 2$. If no valid $K$ exists ($m = 0$), output `-1`.
