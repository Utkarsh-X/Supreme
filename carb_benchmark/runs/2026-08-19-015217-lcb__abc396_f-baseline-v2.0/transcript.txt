### Key Insight
When incrementing $k \to k + 1$, elements $A_i$ such that $B(k)_i = M - 1$ (i.e. $A_i = M - 1 - k$) wrap around from $M - 1$ to $0$. All other elements increase by $1$.

For each element at 0-based index $i$ that wraps around from $M - 1 \to 0$:
- Any non-wrapping element to its right was smaller ($< M-1$) and becomes larger ($> 0$), **reducing** inversions by $1$.
- Any non-wrapping element to its left was smaller ($< M-1$) and becomes larger ($> 0$), **increasing** inversions by $1$.

Summing over all elements $i$ where $A_i = M - 1 - k$, the net change in inversion count transitioning from $k \to k + 1$ is:
$$\Delta(k) = \sum_{i : A_i = M - 1 - k} (2i - N + 1)$$

### Algorithm Structure
1. Compute the initial inversion count for $k = 0$ in $O(N \log M)$ using a Binary Indexed Tree (Fenwick Tree).
2. Precompute `delta_v[v] = sum(2 * i - N + 1)` for each value $v \in [0, M-1]$.
3. Iterate $k$ from $0$ to $M-1$, recording the current inversion count and updating `curr_inv += delta_v[M - 1 - k]`.
