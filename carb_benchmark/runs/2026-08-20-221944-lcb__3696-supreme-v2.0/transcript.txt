### Method Explanation

For a given substring $s[i..j]$ ending at digit $d = \text{int}(s[j]) \in \{1..9\}$:
1. **$d \in \{1, 2, 5\}$**: Every substring ending with $1, 2,$ or $5$ is divisible by $d$. Count is $j + 1$.
2. **$d \in \{3, 6\}$**: Divisibility by $3$ (and by $6$ since any number ending in $6$ is even) depends on the sum of digits modulo $3$. We track frequencies of prefix digit sums modulo $3$.
3. **$d = 9$**: Divisibility depends on sum of digits modulo $9$. We track frequencies of prefix digit sums modulo $9$.
4. **$d = 7$**: $\gcd(10, 7) = 1$ with $10^{-1} \equiv 5 \pmod 7$. We maintain prefix values $P[k]$ modulo $7$ and match normalized prefix remainders $rem_7[k] = (P[k] \times 5^{k+1}) \pmod 7$.
5. **$d = 4$**: $100 \equiv 0 \pmod 4$. Divisibility depends on the last 2 digits $s[j-1..j]$.
6. **$d = 8$**: $1000 \equiv 0 \pmod 8$. Divisibility depends on the last 3 digits $s[j-2..j]$.

- Verified with 500 random stress tests against a brute-force solver.
