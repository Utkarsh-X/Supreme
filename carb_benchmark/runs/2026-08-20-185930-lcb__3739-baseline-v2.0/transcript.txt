### Method Explanation

1. **Problem Formulation**:
   For any pair of distinct grid cells $A$ and $B$, the number of valid arrangements of $k$ pieces containing pieces at both $A$ and $B$ is given by choosing the remaining $k - 2$ pieces out of the remaining $m \cdot n - 2$ cells, which is $\binom{m \cdot n - 2}{k - 2}$.

   Thus, the total sum of Manhattan distances over all valid arrangements is:
   $$\text{Total Sum} = \binom{m \cdot n - 2}{k - 2} \times \sum_{A \neq B} d(A, B) \pmod{10^9 + 7}$$

2. **Sum of Cell Pair Distances**:
   The Manhattan distance between two cells $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is $|x_1 - x_2| + |y_1 - y_2|$.
   Summing this over all pairs of cells decouples into row and column coordinate differences:
   $$\sum_{A \neq B} d(A, B) = n^2 \sum_{0 \le x_1 < x_2 < m} (x_2 - x_1) + m^2 \sum_{0 \le y_1 < y_2 < n} (y_2 - y_1)$$

   Using the combinatorial identity $\sum_{0 \le i < j < L} (j - i) = \binom{L+1}{3} = \frac{(L-1)L(L+1)}{6}$, we compute $S(L) = \frac{(L-1)L(L+1)}{6} \pmod{10^9 + 7}$ efficiently in $O(1)$ time.

3. **Complexity**:
   - **Time Complexity**: $O(m \cdot n)$ to precompute factorials for combinations modulo $10^9+7$, well within the time limit for $m \cdot n \le 10^5$.
   - **Space Complexity**: $O(m \cdot n)$ auxiliary space for storing factorials.
