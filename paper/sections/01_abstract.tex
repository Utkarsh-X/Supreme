\begin{abstract}
Agentic systems can place invariant guidance in the initial context or load procedural guidance progressively. We compare three complete configurations---Supreme, Superpowers by obra, and an unprompted baseline---on all 89 Terminal-Bench 2.1 tasks (267 isolated evaluations), using Gemini 3.6 Flash High through Google's API, with Claude Code as the execution client, at zero temperature. Supreme recorded the highest completion rate (61/89, 68.5\%), followed by Superpowers (58/89, 65.2\%) and Baseline (55/89, 61.8\%). Excluding three upstream environment defects, the corresponding rates were 70.9\%, 67.4\%, and 64.0\%. Supreme also recorded the lowest aggregate wall-clock time (15.15\,h vs.\ 18.30\,h and 16.92\,h) and the lowest modeled cost per successful task under recorded OpenRouter rate assumptions (\$0.489 vs.\ \$0.506 and \$0.497). Baseline had the lowest modeled absolute cost (\$27.33; Superpowers \$29.35; Supreme \$29.80), while recorded token totals were 34.52M for Supreme, 33.95M for Superpowers, and 30.87M for Baseline. On Hard tasks, Supreme passed 20/30 compared with 16/30 for Superpowers and 17/30 for Baseline, a directional lead. Paired McNemar tests did not distinguish aggregate completion rates (all $p > 0.05$). Tool telemetry showed lower immediate background-task polling for Supreme, while Task~\#88 provides a case study of extended exploratory scripting: both Supreme and Superpowers passed, with Superpowers using 11.7 times as many tokens in that run. This evaluation compares complete configurations; it does not isolate guidance delivery from instructional content. The paper presents aggregate results, methods, and analyses; run-level traces and the full evaluation corpus are not part of this release.
\end{abstract}
