\begin{abstract}
Autonomous command-line interface (CLI) agents are increasingly tasked with complex software engineering, systems administration, and algorithmic optimization workflows. A central architectural consideration in agent design is how guidance is structured: embedding invariant engineering principles directly within the initial context versus dynamically loading specialized procedural skills on demand. In this work, we conduct a controlled empirical evaluation across all 89 tasks of the official Terminal-Bench 2.1 benchmark (267 isolated container evaluations) using Gemini 3.6 Flash High within a unified evaluation harness. We evaluate three configurations: Supreme (an invariant constitutional framework), a dynamic skill-loading system based on Superpowers by obra (\texttt{obra/superpowers}), and an unprompted baseline. Although aggregate task success rates do not differ significantly across architectures (Supreme: 68.5\% [61/89]; Superpowers: 65.2\% [58/89]; Baseline: 61.8\% [55/89]; paired McNemar $p > 0.05$; and 70.9\% vs.\ 67.4\% vs.\ 64.0\% after adjusting for three upstream container defects), the architectures exhibit substantial differences in execution behavior, tool ecology, and computational expenditure. On Hard tasks ($N=30$), Supreme demonstrates an increase in provider-reported deliberation tokens (+66.0\%, from 17.0k to 28.3k thinking tokens/task) alongside a directional pass-rate lead (66.7\% vs.\ 53.3\%; paired McNemar $p = 0.22$, exact binomial $p = 0.22$). Transcripts reveal distinct operational failure modes: in open-ended combinatorial tasks (e.g., Task \#88), unconstrained dynamic skill exploration coincided with extensive script-generation loops, writing 116 exploratory files and consuming 3.51 million tokens ($11.7\times$ the tokens of Supreme's 300k-token solve). Furthermore, context-growth accounting indicates that frequent on-demand documentation ingestion accumulated 411.3 million cache read tokens (+115.4 million over baseline), highlighting practical economic trade-offs in multi-turn prompting architectures. We provide complete execution transcripts, evaluation harness configurations, and cryptographic audit ledgers to facilitate independent replication.
\end{abstract}
